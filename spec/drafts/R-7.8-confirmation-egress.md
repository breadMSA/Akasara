# Draft for ASE-0.2 — R-7.8 (confirmation egress)

Status: **draft, not in 0.1.** Proposed insertion point: §7, after R-7.7.
Written 2026-08-22. Rationale evidence: exp-37 (research/alignment,
`run_correction_pairs.py --profile measured`).

---

## The gap this closes

ASE-0.1 §1.2 makes an explicit promise to vendors, at lines 202–204:

> **The labels do not leave at all.** A signal stream without the meaning it was
> recorded for is not a decoder; the recipient still has to collect ground
> truth, per subject — the same cost the vendor paid, not a copy of it.

That is load-bearing and MUST NOT be weakened: it is the reason a vendor can
export T1 without exporting its product. R-7.8 does not touch it. The vendor's
own labels, taxonomy, and training corpus stay inside.

What R-7.8 addresses is a different artefact that 0.1 is silent on: the
**user's own confirmation** — the user saying "that is what I meant" or "no, I
meant this" about a decode of their own signal. It is not the vendor's ground
truth. It is generated by the user, about the user, at use time.

0.1's silence has a specific consequence. R-7.1–R-7.3 guarantee the signal can
reach software the user chooses. Nothing guarantees the *confirmation* can. If
confirmations are only ever recorded inside the vendor's own application, then
the vendor is the only party who can ever pair signal with confirmed meaning,
and the export right delivers a stream that no third party can learn a personal
correction model from — for the reason 0.1 itself states one paragraph earlier.

## Why it matters more than it looks

The pair `(signal, the person's own confirmed meaning)` cannot be bought,
back-filled, or transferred between people: it must be collected from *that
person*, on *that channel*, after the hardware exists. So on the day consumer
read hardware ships, every party's stock of it is zero, and the only thing that
decides who accumulates it is who the user is able to route confirmations to.
R-7.8 is the clause that keeps that decision with the user.

Measured support (exp-37, 2026-08-22, 7 members, unit = member, corruption op
mix measured from real production-ASR ↔ human-corrected pairs): adding
correction pairs alongside sentences beats the matched no-correction-signal
control at every channel quality tested — `mix − halfn` **+0.0064**
CI [+0.0038, +0.0104] at 7.9% CER, **+0.0035** CI [+0.0001, +0.0087] at 11.8%,
and **+0.0336** CI [+0.0142, +0.0516] at 35% (Brain2Qwerty-grade). The absolute
margin grows as the channel worsens, which is the direction a neural channel
sits in. Confirmations are therefore worth most exactly where ASE applies.

## Proposed normative text

**R-7.8 (confirmation egress).** Where a device or its software presents a
decoded result to the user and accepts an indication from the user that the
result was correct or incorrect — including an accept, a reject, an edit, or a
correction the user types or speaks — that indication, together with a reference
to the exported frames it applies to, MUST be obtainable by the user under the
same terms as R-7.1, R-7.3, and R-7.6.

This requirement:

- applies **only** to indications generated by the user about decodes of the
  user's own signal. It creates no obligation regarding the vendor's own labels,
  label taxonomy, training corpus, or annotator output (§1.2, and R-11.5's
  disclosure boundary is unchanged);
- does **not** oblige a vendor to build a confirmation interface. A device that
  never asks the user whether a decode was right is conformant with R-7.8
  vacuously;
- requires only a **correlatable reference** — a frame index, a session id plus
  timestamp range, or an equivalent already carried under §6 — not a copy of the
  frames, and not a stable identifier beyond what the export already contains;
- is satisfied by export on demand (R-7.6). Live streaming of confirmations is
  **not** required.

**R-7.8.1 (no confirmation lock-in).** The device's terms MUST NOT prohibit the
user, or software acting for the user, from using exported confirmations to fit
a model for that user. Restrictions on redistributing another person's
confirmations are permitted and are not affected by this requirement.

Minimum sufficient wording, which a vendor may paste into its terms:

> When you tell the device whether a result was right or wrong, that answer is
> yours. You can export it along with the data it refers to, and use it to
> train something of your own. We place no restriction on that use. This does
> not give you our models, our label sets, or our training data.

> Non-normative: R-7.4 decides whether an interoperability layer between people
> can legally exist. R-7.8 decides whether a personal correction layer can exist
> at all for anyone other than the vendor. They are independent: a device can
> pass R-7.4 and still make itself the only party able to learn what its user
> meant. The asymmetry R-7.8 removes is not about data volume — confirmations
> are per-person and do not aggregate across users — it is about whether the
> loop can be run outside the vendor's application.

## Open questions before this goes into 0.2

1. **Does it belong in ASE at all?** ASE is a signal-export spec; a confirmation
   is not a signal. The counter-argument is that R-7.4 is already a terms
   clause rather than a wire-format clause, so §7 has precedent for governing
   what the vendor may forbid rather than what it must emit.
2. **Vendor cost.** R-7.8 is cheap for a vendor that already logs confirmations
   and expensive for one that discards them. The vacuous-conformance carve-out
   is what keeps it from being a build mandate, but it also lets a vendor evade
   it by never asking — which may make the clause toothless in practice.
3. **Privacy inversion.** A confirmation is a statement of intent and is more
   sensitive than the raw frame. Making it exportable makes it exfiltratable.
   R-7.3's per-application consent applies, but this should be stated, not
   assumed.
4. **Not yet reviewed against** R-11.5's disclosure clauses or the conformance
   suite (§11). Nothing here is testable by the current suite.
